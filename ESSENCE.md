# The application — essence

**What it is:** A shared decision artifact that helps a team discover, evaluate, and prioritize AI use cases together — replacing a spreadsheet as the center of that conversation. Core promise: *after using it, a group can identify and agree on an evidence-backed next AI use case to pursue.*

**Guiding stance:** AI assists (drafts, challenges, surfaces gaps); the team always decides. AI suggestions must be visibly distinct from team-authored content, and AI can never silently change values, rankings, selections, confirmations, or archiving.

## The mental model

- **Workspace** — one team's shared home; holds a North Star and one living Map of opportunities.
- **North Star** — the Workspace's **AI vision** (enduring principle) and **AI strategy** (current priorities). A compass to guide exploration, never a gate that blocks it.
- **Opportunity Map** — the living, never-locked board of a team's AI opportunities ("Islands"). Always editable, even after decisions are made.
- **Scouting note** — a lightweight, individually-authored early idea. Not yet a claimed opportunity. Can later be transferred into an Island, preserving the note as visible provenance.
- **Island** — one AI opportunity, with a **Chart Room** (its deep-dive workspace) and adjustable **evaluation values**.
- **Chart Room** — where the team develops the opportunity hypothesis: today's workflow/problem → AI-enabled change → possible outcome, plus evidence, unknowns, safeguards/risk, and ownership. AI can help organize or question it, but only the team authors it.
- **Evaluation value** — a small, adjustable rubric per Island (e.g. value, readiness, effort) that drives ranking, and must stay explainable — never a black box.
- **Expedition** — a confirmed, time-bound commitment to explore a chosen set of Islands next. The team sets a planning horizon and a weighting **lens**, gets a ranked shortlist, picks Islands, and writes an **Island Charter** for each (what to learn/do next). Only one Expedition is active at a time; a new one supersedes the prior.
- **Map snapshot / Map changes** — confirming an Expedition takes an immutable snapshot of the Map. Later, the current Map can be compared against that snapshot — new notes/Islands, changed values, archived Islands, or an unselected Island now outranking a selected one — shown as a calm, read-only notice that never auto-re-ranks or rewrites the Expedition.
- **Archived Island** — removed from the active Map with a reason; reversible.

Retired/legacy language to avoid reintroducing: Iteration, Decision frame, Map focus, Shortlist, Next Opportunity decision, "Pilot" (as a synonym for Expedition).

*(Note: this exploration/map metaphor is the current product's language, not the app's name — worth deciding afresh whether to keep this metaphor at all when starting over.)*

## Product principles worth preserving in a rebuild

- The team owns strategic judgement; AI is an assistant, not a decision-maker.
- Context, evidence, and evaluation rationale must be explicit, inspectable, and comparable across opportunities.
- The artifact persists and is meant to be shared with stakeholders, not just used in one workshop.
- Start from a best-practice default rubric, but let the team configure and reweight it live.
- An opportunity earns deeper detail only after it survives early screening — don't force full rigor on every raw idea.
- History is captured via snapshots and comparisons, not by freezing or versioning the whole artifact.

## Deliberately deferred ideas (still valid future backlog, not MVP)

Importing existing documents/spreadsheets/notes; tracking real-world outcomes after an idea becomes an implementation initiative; AI-assisted blind-spot discovery (duplicates, missing areas, weak assumptions); multiple saved named lenses (e.g. "quick wins" vs "strategic bets"); a two-axis visual opportunity map; full real-time collaborative editing with presence/cursors.
